The Problem Statement

Poverty is not just an income problem.
It is an infrastructure problem.

Across Kenya and much of Africa, millions of people work every day. They operate businesses, contribute to savings groups, manage community organizations, conserve forests, and generate immense economic value. Yet, many remain financially vulnerable.

The World Bank estimates that, without meaningful change, millions more could fall into poverty by the end of 2026. This is not because people aren't working hard. The problem is that the financial systems surrounding them were never designed to help them capture, preserve, and compound the value they create. They capture transactions, but they fail to build wealth.

"Today's financial infrastructure excels at moving money but does very little to help ordinary people turn transactions into long-term wealth. A payment is treated as the end of the journey, rather than the beginning of a wealth-building process."

The Structural Failures

Current financial systems are built on four deep structural flaws that keep everyday users trapped in cycles of liquidity constraints and wealth erosion.

FAILURE 01

Payments Do Not Automatically Create Wealth

Kenyans have some of the world's most successful digital payment systems (like mobile money). They make sending and receiving money simple. However, once a transaction is complete, the system stops.

The Fragmentation of Finance

Today’s financial journey is divided across disconnected systems. You receive payments on one platform, save on another, and need a completely different provider for insurance, pensions, or digital assets. These systems rarely communicate. Instead of one seamless financial journey, people are forced to navigate multiple fragmented platforms, creating unnecessary costs and missed opportunities.

The Missed Opportunity

A business transaction does not automatically improve access to capital. Savings do not automatically move into inflation-resistant assets. Every day, people transact, but every transaction becomes a missed opportunity for financial empowerment.

FAILURE 02

Financial Products Ignore How Communities Operate

Most financial technology is strictly designed for the isolated individual. But in reality, African economies are built around communities.

The Reality of Collective Trust

A single person may simultaneously be an SME owner, belong to a SACCO or Chama, participate in a Community Forest Association (CFA), and support a CBO. These groups work collectively, save collectively, and make financial decisions together.

The Consequence

Because very few financial platforms are designed specifically for their way of operating, these communities rely on manual ledgers and fragmented tools. There is no technical infrastructure enabling them to enforce savings rules, automate distributions, or verify fund utilization without relying solely on the fragile integrity of human administrators.

FAILURE 03

Valuable Assets Remain Trapped

Millions of people and communities already own valuable assets, but traditional financial systems fail to recognize them, leaving them economically underutilized.

SMEs are Asset-Rich, Liquidity-Poor

Small businesses generate sales, inventory, and transaction histories every day. Yet, because traditional banking requires formal real-estate collateral and legacy credit histories, these daily digital business records rarely translate into improved access to liquidity or working capital.

CFAs and Ecological Data

Community Forest Associations produce valuable environmental data and manage critical natural resources. However, existing conservation monitoring tools are often costly, technically complex, or designed for large multinational organizations. This limits grassroots communities' ability to generate and capture value from their own conservation work, keeping them perpetually dependent on grants.

FAILURE 04

Modern Finance (Web3) Remains Inaccessible

Blockchain technology has introduced new ways to save, invest, own digital assets, and coordinate financial systems without intermediaries. Yet, it is failing the people who need it most.

The Usability Gap

For most people, Web3 remains too technical. Wallets, private seed keys, fluctuating gas fees, multiple blockchains, and complex terminology create an intimidating learning curve. It assumes a technically literate user who does not need financial inclusion in the first place.

The Resulting Exclusion

Many young Africans want exposure to these opportunities, but the user experience prevents adoption. Millions remain excluded from technologies (like tokenized real-world assets or stablecoins) that could immediately improve their long-term financial resilience.

Who is Bearing the Cost?

Four interconnected demographics are actively participating in the digital economy, yet failing to build wealth due to this infrastructure gap.

SMEs: Operating Blind to Capital

The Reality: SMEs in Africa generate massive daily transaction volumes. They move inventory, pay suppliers, and receive customer payments digitally.

The Problem: Despite this digital footprint, traditional finance ignores it. A mobile money payment proves cash flow, but it doesn't build a credit score. Therefore, SMEs face a massive financing gap. They hold value in inventory and business operations, but that value is difficult to finance, divide, transfer, or use as collateral.

The Impact: Wealth exists, but it remains locked. Businesses cannot scale because they cannot borrow against their own proven digital success.

CFAs: Stewards Without Reward

The Reality: Community Forest Associations are on the frontlines of global conservation. They protect trees, manage natural resources, and perform critical ecological work every day.

The Problem: They lack affordable, purpose-built conservation monitoring and data management tools. The global carbon and ecological asset markets operate on verifiable data, but generating that data requires expensive MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification) infrastructure.

The Impact: CFAs produce value that the whole world benefits from, but because they cannot efficiently digitize and tokenize this work, they cannot trade it. They remain reliant on unpredictable external grants instead of building sustainable community wealth.

Chamas: Trust Without Infrastructure

The Reality: Communities have already demonstrated a strong culture of saving and mutual support. Chamas and SACCOs pool millions of dollars collectively.

The Problem: Savings alone is no longer enough. Inflation steadily reduces the value of idle money. Furthermore, because these groups lack automated technical infrastructure (like smart contracts), they rely entirely on human administrators, making them vulnerable to mismanagement and fraud.

The Impact: Communities possess massive pooled capital, but cannot safely deploy it into diversified, inflation-resistant investment products, limiting their ability to create generational wealth.

Young Africans: Excluded from Web3

The Reality: Africa's youth are digitally native, entrepreneurial, and highly aware of global financial opportunities, including cryptocurrency and decentralized finance.

The Problem: The bridge between their daily reality (mobile money) and Web3 is broken. The complexity barrier (seed phrases, gas fees) and fee unpredictability make everyday use impossible. For small transactions, network fees can exceed the transaction value.

The Impact: An entire generation is left relying on depreciating local fiat currencies, unable to access the very tools designed to protect purchasing power.

The Quantifiable Damage

The failure to transition from moving money to building wealth has resulted in severe, measurable economic consequences.

Financial Exclusion
~57%

Of Sub-Saharan African adults remain unbanked or underbanked. Traditional banks require physical branches, minimum deposits, and formal IDs—conditions most informal workers cannot satisfy.

Wealth Erasure
Double
Digit

Annual inflation rates erode local currency savings across major African markets. Holding local currency in standard bank accounts is functionally a loss-making activity for the majority of savers.

The Capital Gap
$330B+

The estimated annual SME financing gap. Because transaction histories on payment apps don't translate to credit, businesses are starved of the capital they clearly generate.

The Real Problem

The problem is not simply poverty. The problem is not simply inflation. The problem is not simply financial exclusion.

The real problem is that today's financial infrastructure captures economic activity but fails to transform that activity into long-term wealth creation.

Millions of Africans already create value every single day. They simply lack an integrated financial system that helps them preserve that value, grow it, and pass it on to future generations.
